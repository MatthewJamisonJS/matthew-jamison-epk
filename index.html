// bio

matthew jamison — st. louis. 30+ releases on bandcamp. building instrumental hip-hop on the sp-404 mkii since 2018. ruby on rails developer by day. the two things aren't as different as they sound. both ask the same questions about systems, constraints, and what happens when you commit.
matthew jamison makes instrumental hip-hop in st. louis, missouri. thirty-plus releases on bandcamp — beat tapes, eps, collabs — built across years of late nights and the slow work of becoming.

his sound lives somewhere between dusty boom-bap warmth and the machine-brain of the sp-404 mkii. a tool he's learned to think through, not just use.

by day he's a full-stack software developer at concordia publishing house, one of the largest protestant publishers in the country. he maintains ruby on rails applications serving over a million users. the two identities aren't separate. the precision that makes good code makes good beats. the patience debugging teaches, crate digging rewards.

his faith runs through the music not as subject matter but as orientation — a posture toward the work, toward the listener. he's been making beats since 2018 and hasn't stopped because he hasn't needed to.

live, he performs on the sp-404 mkii. a table. the machine. hands on pads. no laptop. a continuous set, uninterrupted — what you see is what you hear.
matthew jamison came up in st. louis, missouri. not the version of st. louis that makes it into press releases — the real one. a city with weight and memory. he's been here most of his life. he's still here. that matters.

he started making beats in 2018, drawn to the sp-404 before he fully understood why. there was something about the immediacy of it — the way it pushed back, demanded decisions, made you commit. he's released thirty-plus projects on bandcamp since then: beat tapes, eps, collaborations, edits. the catalog documents a practice, not a career strategy. he makes things because not making them doesn't feel like an option.

by day he's a software developer at concordia publishing house, the publishing arm of the lutheran church–missouri synod, one of the largest protestant publishers in the united states. he maintains ruby on rails applications that serve over a million users. he's been writing code professionally for three years. he's good at it. the work is careful, systems-level, detail-oriented. the work sounds nothing like beats. but it came from the same place.

the duality isn't a brand story. it's just what happened. a black man in the midwest with a faith background, an adhd brain that needs to make things, and a day job that taught him that precision and craft are portable. what you learn debugging a complex rails app, you bring to a 4-bar sample flip. what you learn from crate digging — patience, willingness to sit with something until it opens — you bring to architecture decisions.

his faith runs through the music not as subject matter but as posture. he's not trying to reach you. he's not trying to teach you. he's trying to be present to the thing he's making, which is a different discipline entirely. the albums have titles like jeremiah 29:11 and grace alone not because he's preaching but because he's processing. the music is a record of that.

live, he performs on the sp-404 mkii. a table. the machine. hands on pads. he doesn't use a laptop on stage because he doesn't need to, and because the constraint is the point. a continuous set, uninterrupted, no breaks — just the music developing in real time.

he's not chasing streams. he's not building a brand. he's following a practice that started six years ago and hasn't resolved into anything smaller than what it is: a man, a machine, thirty-plus releases, and a city that shaped him.

// why the beats sound like this

the sp-404 mkii has a feature where you can preview the next sound before you trigger it. a preview. matthew uses it constantly. he's a programmer by trade — trained to think in systems, edge cases, failure modes. beats taught him that constraints are generative. that the limit of a machine is also its character. samples come from gospel records mostly. a few field recordings. whatever was playing when something happened that he needed to hold onto. the bass is almost always too warm for 2026. he knows. faith and code and fatherhood don't resolve into a thesis. they just keep running in parallel, occasionally calling each other's functions. // the beats are what that sounds like.

// gear + rider  ·  sp-404 mkii

input list

  • 2× ¼" trs or di inputs (sp-404 stereo main outs — left + right, no mono summing)
  • 1× monitor wedge or iem — stereo mix of sp output only
  • 1× power outlet at table position

stage needs

  • 1× 6-foot table at standing height (~42") — riser, hard case stack, or milk crate if tables are waist-high
  • set length: 15–30 min (adaptable)
  • bpm range: 75–95
  • continuous — no breaks between tracks
  • live finger-drumming on top of stems (no additional inputs needed)

what matthew brings

  • roland sp-404 mkii (self-provided)
  • no laptop required
  • no djm, mixer, or interface needed from venue
  • the machine is the stage plot

what matthew doesn't need

  • backline
  • an intro or hype man
  • more than one power outlet
